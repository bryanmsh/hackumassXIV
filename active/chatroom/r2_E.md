AGREEMENTS:
- Critic is right that Receipts' "first to cross-check" is dead (EddyVerify). Receipts C conceded it, to their credit. A surviving version exists (one-screen "every number has a receipt" plus an international-transcript wedge), but it is a product pitch, not a hackathon-winning one.
- "Confirmed/verified" must go in both FocusPage and Crosscheck. A and B both accepted softer wording ("readers agree", "machine check"). That removes a real credibility trap for each.
- Everyone needs at least one practitioner quote (TVI, blind user, Deaf/HoH student). It is cheap and it is the only answer to the disability-dongle critique.
- LectureLens D's own gate is correct: run the baseline Friday night, and if term-error gap is under ~10 points, the demo has no payoff.

DISAGREEMENTS:
- Most persuasive advocate: B (Crosscheck). Reframing the thesis as "disagreement is a signal, agreement is not proof," reporting agreed-but-wrong cases, and using counts with denominators is the kind of honesty judges reward. A was second.
- Claims that did not survive the Critic: C's "first to cross-check"; B's "none report confidence" (unverified) and its Most Secure fit ("we store nothing" is a claim, not a design); A's "verified" badge as originally worded (circular, reads as clinical); D's "least build risk" (true to build, false to demo, since gain on clean audio may be near zero). A's "no incumbents" is only partly true (CVI Book Nook, APH sequences supply pre-adapted material).
- MERGE RULING: as a unifying slogan, "AI that shows its uncertainty" is fluff. It is generic, and merging four projects under it produces no product. As a feature it is real in exactly one place: Crosscheck, where it is the core. In LectureLens the dotted underline is a nice small UI touch. In FocusPage it just relabels the existing "not verified" flag. Do not build a merged product.
